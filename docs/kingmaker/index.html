How this is calculated

Feasible coalitions. A coalition is any group of parties that are not Out, with no Left party alongside a Right party. It wins if it holds a majority of the House: at least half the seats plus one, so a 65–65 tie is not a win.

Power. Power is the Banzhaf power index. Count every feasible winning coalition. A member is critical if the coalition loses its majority without it. A party's power is its share of all critical appearances, so the figures add to 100%. The ×n beside each figure is power divided by seat share.

Minimal winning coalitions. Coalitions in which every party is critical. These are the realistic governments.

Kingmaker. The party with the most power. When parties tie, the smallest of them is shown, since it gets the most power per seat.

Preliminary and final. Preliminary seats are from the Electoral Commission's election-night count (7 November: a House of 131). Final seats are from the official result after special votes (27 November: 130). Each change marker compares the selected count with the other one.

This counts arithmetic only. It doesn't weigh policy distance, ministerial demands or confidence-and-supply deals.
